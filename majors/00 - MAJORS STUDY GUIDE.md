3. **Michel Foucault** → readings on Moodle

Not on the Majors: Socrates, Plato (Apology, Republic), Aristotle, Machiavelli.

## Exam pattern (40 marks)
- **Section A – 20 MCQs (1 mark each)**
  - 75% (≈15 Qs): **Rawls, Foucault, Arendt**
  - 25% (≈5 Qs): Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau together
- **Section B – Subjective (20 marks)**: answer **any 2** questions (10 marks each); equal weight to all 6 thinkers.
- The announcement says to prepare from *all readings provided on Moodle*.

## Concepts the course outline (July) flags for each thinker
Use this as a checklist while reading. It is not extra reading.
- **Hobbes:** the sovereign, state of nature, authority and social order (*Leviathan*)
- **Locke:** consent; **property** (*Second Treatise*, ch. V)
- **Rousseau:** nature → society; the "contents and discontents" of freedom (*Emile*)
- **Foucault:** punishment as deterrence, reform and retribution; panopticism
- **Rawls:** justice as fairness, distributive justice, **original position, veil of ignorance** (Nozick mentioned as a contrast)
- **Arendt:** banality of evil (covered in the 14 Nov online class)

The outline's "world thinkers" list (Gandhi, Ambedkar, Sen, Fanon, Mbembe, Fukuyama, Chomsky, etc.) is **not** on the Majors.
The 9 Nov syllabus announcement replaced it with the 6 thinkers above.

## Study order
1. Hobbes → Locke → Rousseau: quick revision (slides 1b, 2b)
2. Rawls (4b, 4a)
3. Foucault (5a + video)
4. Arendt (6a + video)
5. Nelson chapters in full (1a, 2a, 3a)
6. Practise linking thinkers in answers

Links: Hobbes → Locke → Rousseau → Rawls (consent) · Hobbes ↔ Foucault ↔ Arendt (power, obedience)


Marks per thinker (approx.): Rawls / Foucault / Arendt ≈ 5 MCQs each + ⅙ of Section B.
Hobbes / Locke / Rousseau ≈ 1–2 MCQs each + ⅙ of Section B.

| Step | What | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hobbes → Locke → Rousseau, quick pass** (slides 1b, 2b; skim 3a) | Already done for Quiz 2. Each reacts to the previous one, and Rawls builds on all three. |
| 2 | **Rawls** (4b then 4a) | Highest weight. Original position = a hypothetical state of nature; veil of ignorance = fair consent. 4b opens with "never signed a social contract". |
| 3 | **Foucault** (5a + Panopticon video) | Highest weight. Inverts Hobbes: visible sovereign → invisible disciplinary power. Rousseau's "chains" become internalised. |
| 4 | **Arendt** (6a + final speech video) | Highest weight. Eichmann as an obedient cog in a bureaucratic machine follows from Foucault's normalised, disciplined subject; also challenges Hobbesian obedience. |
| 5 | **Nelson chapters in full** (1a, 2a, 3a) | Depth for Section B essays. |
| 6 | **Comparison practice** | Section B rewards linking thinkers (see the threads below). |

Threads to use in essays:
- **Consent and legitimacy:** Hobbes (fear) → Locke (consent, property) → Rousseau (general will) → Rawls (hypothetical fair consent)
- **Power and the subject:** Hobbes's sovereign ↔ Foucault's discipline ↔ Arendt's bureaucracy and thoughtlessness
- **Obedience and responsibility:** Hobbes (obey the sovereign) vs Arendt (Eichmann "just obeyed the law")
- **Freedom:** Locke (natural rights) → Rousseau (chains, civil freedom) → Foucault (freedom shaped by norms) → Rawls (equal basic liberties)

For Section B, prepare at least 3 thinkers in depth (e.g. Rawls, Foucault, Arendt) and one contract theorist as backup.
