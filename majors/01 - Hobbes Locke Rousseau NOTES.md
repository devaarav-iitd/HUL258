# Hobbes · Locke · Rousseau: Short Notes
Source: Nelson ch. 8–10 + TA tutorial slides (Anu Panging)

## 1. Thomas Hobbes (1588–1679), *Leviathan* (1651)
**Context:** English Civil War. Backed absolute monarchy but **not divine right**, so he offended both Puritans and Royalists.

- **Method:** copies Galilean science and **geometry**. Uses the **resolutive–compositive method** (break things into parts, then rebuild by deduction).
- **Materialism:** reality = **bodies and motion**. Sensations are **phantasms** (motions in the body).
- **Nominalism:** no universals, only particulars. Words, not things, are true or false.
- **Human nature (utilitarian psychology):** driven by pleasure and pain (**appetite / aversion**).
  - **Will** = "the last appetite in deliberating."
  - **Felicity** = endless progress of desire from one object to the next.
  - "A perpetual and restless desire of **power after power**, that ceaseth only in death."
- **State of nature:** **absolute liberty + absolute equality** → everyone has a **right to everything** → **state of war** (war of all against all). Life is constant fear and anxiety.
- **Way out:** fear of death + reason → **laws of nature** (seek peace; give up your right to all things).
- **Contract:** made **between subjects only**: "I **authorize** and give up my right of governing myself to this man…"
  - The **sovereign is NOT a party** to the contract → its power is **absolute, undivided, unlimited**.
  - The state = an **artificial / fictitious person** (like a corporation) = the **Leviathan**.
- **Legacy (Nelson):** *not* the father of totalitarianism. Ironically, he laid the base for the liberal **negative state**. IR **realists** use "power after power" between states.

## 2. John Locke (1632–1704), *Two Treatises of Government*, *Letter Concerning Toleration*
**Context:** Puritan family, influenced by the **Levellers**. Returned after the **Glorious Revolution (1688)**.
The *First Treatise* refutes **Filmer's *Patriarcha*** (divine right, king's power = a father's **paternal power**, from Adam).

- **Knowledge:** empiricist. The mind is a **tabula rasa** (blank slate). All knowledge comes from **sensation**.
  → **toleration** → **separation of church and state**.
- **Human nature:** also utilitarian (pleasure/pain), but less extreme than Hobbes.
  Puzzle: how can pleasure-seekers live under *limited* government?
- **State of nature, two views (Nelson calls this an ambiguity):**
  1. **Peaceful:** perfect freedom and equality, governed by **natural law** (reason). Not the same as a state of war (against Hobbes).
  2. **Violent:** endless desire for property → it becomes a **state of war**.
- **Natural rights:** life, liberty, estate = **"property"**. You have **property in your own person**.
- **Labour theory of property:** mixing your labour with nature makes a thing yours.
  - **Limits:** leave **"enough and as good"** for others; **spoilage** (100 bushels of apples, half rot = "robb'd others").
  - **Money** (shells, pebbles) is accepted by **tacit consent**. It doesn't spoil → **unlimited accumulation** (Macpherson: **possessive individualism**).
- **Two-stage contract:**
  1. **Social contract** → **society** (needs **unanimous** consent)
  2. **Political "contract"** → **government** (needs only **majority** consent). This is really a **fiduciary trust**: government is a **trustee**, not a master.
  - If the government is dissolved, society survives.
- **Consent:** **express** (formal) vs **tacit** (by owning property or using the benefits of society).
- **Government:** **limited** and **constitutional**.
  - **Legislative** is supreme, but the majority means men of property and it shouldn't sit constantly.
  - **Executive + federative** (foreign affairs) powers. **Prerogative** = the executive acting at its discretion for the public good.
  - **Separation of powers** → checks and balances.
- **Right of revolution / popular sovereignty:** the people can overthrow a government that violates natural rights.
- **Legacy:** father of **liberalism** and the **negative state**, but not a modern democrat (property franchise).
- *TA's critique (good for an essay):* the two views aren't really different. Both rest on the same pleasure/pain psychology, and choosing between them is a theoretical decision, not something evidence can settle.

## 3. Jean-Jacques Rousseau (1712–1778), *First & Second Discourses*, *Social Contract* (1762), *Emile*
**"Man is born free, and everywhere he is in chains."** The last of the great contract theorists.

- **First Discourse** (Arts & Sciences, 1750): civilisation has corrupted morals. He attacks **perverted reason**, not reason itself.
- **Second Discourse** (Inequality): **private property → inequality → loss of freedom**. Still, he does not abolish property and is not a proto-Marxist.
- **Critique of Hobbes and Locke:** "they spoke about savage man and they described civil man."
  - Locke projects civilised **reason** onto natural man.
  - Hobbes projects civilised **passions**: power after power is a *product* of civilisation.
- **Natural man:** naturally good. Has **self-love** + **pity/compassion** (the *only natural virtue*). He is **not a primitivist** and does not prefer the "noble savage."
- **Contract:** **total alienation** of each person's rights **to the whole community** (not to one ruler, as in Hobbes) → everyone's condition is equal.
- **General will** (common interest, always right) vs **will of all** (sum of private wills) vs **particular will** (self-interest).
  - "The people are never corrupted, but they are often deceived."
- **"Forced to be free":** whoever disobeys the general will can be compelled to obey it. This makes sense only under **positive liberty** (freedom *for* a moral end).
- **Three liberties:** **natural** liberty → **civil** liberty → **moral liberty** ("which alone renders a man master of himself").
- **Popular sovereignty:** sovereignty belongs to the whole people and cannot be represented. He attacks Locke's representative legislature for putting sovereignty in a small minority.
- **Government:** democracy is not ideal (it mixes legislative and executive). **Elective aristocracy** is best (for middle-sized states). Admits all governments are somewhat **mixed**.
- **Supporting institutions:**
  - **Legislator**
  - **Civil religion** ("religion of the citizen" vs Christianity's otherworldliness)
  - **Censorship**: **opinion** is the most fundamental law, "written in the heart"
  - **Patriotism** unites self-love with love of others
- **Legacy:** labelled everything from **liberal to totalitarian**. Nelson says the totalitarian risks are softened by the small, democratic polity he wants.

## Comparison Table

| | **Hobbes** | **Locke** | **Rousseau** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key work | *Leviathan* | *Two Treatises* | *Social Contract*, *Discourses* |
| Knowledge / method | Materialism, geometry, nominalism | Empiricism, tabula rasa | Critique of Enlightenment reason |
| Human nature | Selfish, power after power | Rational, but property-hungry | Naturally good (self-love + pity) |
| State of nature | **War** of all against all | **Peace** under natural law (view 1) → war (view 2) | Peaceful, free, equal |
| What goes wrong | Fear, competition | No impartial judge; property and money conflicts | **Private property** → inequality → chains |
| Contract between | Subjects only | All (society), then majority (government) | Each with **all** (the community) |
| Stages | One | **Two** (society, then government as trust) | One (total alienation) |
| Sovereign | One man or assembly, **not a party** | **People**; government = trustee | **People** (general will) |
| Sovereign power | **Absolute**, undivided | **Limited**, separation of powers | Absolute *but* belongs to the people |
| Liberty | Absence of restraint; liberty where the law is silent | **Negative** liberty (natural rights) | **Positive / moral** liberty |
| Property | Created by the sovereign | **Natural right** (labour) | Source of inequality |
| Right to resist | Essentially none | **Yes**, right of revolution | Disobeyer is "forced to be free" |
| Religion | State controls it | **Toleration**, church–state separation | **Civil religion** |
| Best government | Absolute monarchy | Constitutional, legislative supreme | **Elective aristocracy** |
| Label | Absolutist (not totalitarian) | Father of **liberalism** | Liberal ↔ totalitarian (debated) |

## Special Terms (MCQ list)

**Hobbes**
- **Resolutive–compositive method:** break a thing into parts, then rebuild by deduction
- **Materialism:** only bodies in motion exist
- **Phantasm:** a sensation or image = motion in the body
- **Nominalism:** no universals, only names and particulars
- **Appetite / aversion:** motion toward / away from objects
- **Will:** "the last appetite in deliberating"
- **Felicity:** continual progress of desire
- **Power after power:** restless desire that ends only in death
- **State of nature / state of war:** absolute liberty and equality → war of all against all
- **Right of nature / right to everything:** in the state of nature, everyone can do anything
- **Laws of nature:** rules of reason (1st: seek peace; 2nd: give up your right to all things)
- **Covenant / authorization:** "I authorize and give up my right of governing myself…"
- **Sovereign / subject:** the sovereign is not a party to the contract
- **Leviathan / artificial (fictitious) person / commonwealth:** the state

**Locke**
- **Tabula rasa:** blank-slate mind
- **Sensation (and reflection):** the sources of knowledge
- **Toleration:** *Letter Concerning Toleration*
- **Filmer's *Patriarcha* / paternal power / divine right:** what the *First Treatise* refutes
- **Natural law / natural rights:** life, liberty, estate = **property**
- **Property in one's person**
- **Labour theory of property**
- **"Enough and as good"**
- **Spoilage limitation:** the apples example
- **Money:** tacit consent → unlimited accumulation
- **Possessive individualism:** Macpherson's reading
- **Two-stage contract:** social contract (unanimous) → government (majority)
- **Fiduciary trust / trustee**
- **Express vs tacit consent**
- **Legislative (supreme), executive, federative powers**
- **Prerogative:** executive discretion for the public good
- **Separation of powers / limited government / negative state**
- **Right of revolution / popular sovereignty / Glorious Revolution / Levellers**

**Rousseau**
- **"Everywhere in chains"**
- **First Discourse:** Arts & Sciences, Academy of Dijon
- **Second Discourse:** Inequality
- **"Savage man vs civil man":** his critique of Hobbes and Locke
- **Self-love, pity/compassion:** the only natural virtue
- **"Noble savage":** he does *not* prefer it
- **Private property → inequality**
- **Total alienation**
- **General will / will of all / particular will**
- **"Forced to be free"**
- **Positive vs negative liberty**
- **Natural, civil, moral liberty**
- **Popular sovereignty**, sovereignty not representable
- **Elective aristocracy / mixed government**
- **Legislator, civil religion ("religion of the citizen"), censorship, opinion, patriotism**
- ***Emile*, *Discourse on Political Economy*, *Government of Poland***
